From Web Developer to Unity AR/VR Developer: Magic Joey, Human Anatomy AR and Industrial VR

How I started my career building websites with WordPress, PHP and JavaScript, then moved into Unity AR/VR development: Magic Joey AR learning cards, a Human Anatomy AR app and an industrial VR project.

By Sachin Korde, Unity Developer, Pune 2 min read 15 Nov 2026
UnityARVRC#WordPressPHP

My career started at Augment Works in Pune, a company focused on AR and VR. I joined as a web developer and left as a Unity AR/VR developer. This is how that happened and what I worked on.

Starting with the web

My first projects were client websites. I built many of them using WordPress, HTML, CSS, JavaScript and jQuery, and sometimes wrote the backend in PHP with CodeIgniter. Working directly with clients taught me to gather requirements, explain technical choices simply and deliver what people actually need.

Moving into Unity

Then I got the chance to work in Unity on the company's AR/VR products, and I never looked back. Three projects shaped me:

  • Magic Joey: AR learning cards for kids. Children scan printed cards, such as alphabets, fruits, vehicles and animals, and watch them come alive as interactive 3D models with audio and activities.
  • Human Anatomy AR: an interactive AR app for exploring the human body in 3D, which makes anatomy easier to understand than flat diagrams.
  • An industrial VR project: an immersive virtual reality experience for an industrial client.

What web development gave me as a game developer

  • Client communication: understanding what the customer really wants before building.
  • API thinking: later, connecting Unity apps to REST APIs felt natural.
  • Shipping mindset: websites have deadlines, and that habit of finishing carried over to games.

Tech stack

Unity, C#, augmented reality, virtual reality, WordPress, HTML, CSS, JavaScript, jQuery, PHP, CodeIgniter.
