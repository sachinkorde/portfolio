Joining a Large Unity Codebase: My Work on Cadaviz, a Virtual Dissection Table

How I got up to speed on the large Cadaviz Unity codebase on my own and designed a new section for the Male Full Body module with a clean, modular class structure.

By Sachin Korde, Unity Developer, Pune 2 min read 15 Nov 2026
Unity 3DC#OOPMedical 3D

Cadaviz is an advanced virtual human dissection table with more than 90 installations, used by medical colleges for interactive 3D anatomy learning. At ImmersiveVision Technology I added a new section to its Male Full Body module.

The real challenge: a large, unfamiliar codebase

Adding a feature to a mature product is very different from starting a new project. Before writing any code, I had to understand how Cadaviz already worked.

  • I studied the project on my own: its architecture, how data flows through it, and its coding conventions.
  • I traced how existing sections were built, so my new section would fit naturally instead of being bolted on.

Building the new section

Once I understood the structure, I designed and implemented the new section in the Male Full Body module. I organised it with a clean, modular class structure, so each part has a clear job and the next developer can extend it easily. The 3D models and medical content were provided by the company's content team, and my job was to integrate them correctly into the product.

Lessons that apply to any team

  • Read before you write. Time spent understanding existing code pays off in fewer bugs and faster reviews.
  • Match the codebase's style. Consistency matters more than personal preference in a shared project.
  • Keep classes small and focused. It makes content-heavy features much easier to maintain.

Tech stack

Unity 3D, C#, object-oriented design, 3D model and content integration.
